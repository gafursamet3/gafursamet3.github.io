MOSFET Applications in Analog Design

This laboratory project focuses on the practical implementation, simulation, and measurement of three fundamental analog circuit topologies using the BS108 n-channel MOSFET.

Part 1: MOSFET as a Switch

The MOSFET was driven by a 3.3V PWM signal (50% duty cycle, 20ns rise/fall time) to control a 50mA load. The primary objective was simulating and measuring the transient power loss during switching intervals by plotting the function p(t) = v(t) * i(t) over a single period.

Part 2: Current Mirror

A dual-MOSFET n-channel current mirror was designed to transfer an input current to a constant load path. The circuit was dimensioned to achieve a targeted output current of 10mA. Subsequent load resistance variations were simulated to model the current mirror as a real power source, calculating its short circuit current (I0) and internal resistance (Ri).

Part 3: Single-Ended Amplifier

A common-source amplifier was engineered to achieve a specific voltage amplification of Au = -20. The design required precise operating point configuration using the MOSFET's transfer function and output characteristic field, calculating appropriate values for the drain, source, and gate bias resistors. The input signal (1kHz sine wave) was measured against the output to analyze signal distortion and verify the amplification factor.
